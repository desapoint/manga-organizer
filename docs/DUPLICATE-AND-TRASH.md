# Duplicate review and managed trash

This specification owns expanded duplicate review and the F34 managed-trash lifecycle. F32 covers classification, F70 owns the review workflow, and F33/F69 provide storage safety and reviewed mutation. Keep the existing exact-copy path until the managed-trash replacement is qualified. [Current file operations](FILE-ORGANIZATION.md)

## Findings and persistent alternatives

Derive groups from indexed locations, physical keys, verified content hashes, release and chapter links, and versioned evidence. A group may have several labels and overlapping subgroups. Use reason codes, evidence references and confidence for each relationship. Keep unavailable paths, unresolved mappings and stale analysis visible with their state. Recompute affected findings on scan, release edit and coverage correction.

| Evidence | Findings and treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Physical identity plus whole-file digest | Distinguish independently stored byte-identical copies, hardlink aliases and unknown physical identity. Only a separately verified copy qualifies as a retained exact survivor. |
| Ordered strong page hashes and verified coverage | Compare equivalent pages in different packaging or recompression. Record changed pages, ordering, bonus pages and missing matches. Treat resolution or quality comparisons as evidence, with perceptual similarity a candidate for review. |
| Release metadata and canonical coverage | Show translation, language, group, publication edition, black-and-white or color, censorship, revisions or corrections, and quality differences together. Display partial overlap among standalone chapters, volumes and collections, including unknown or unresolved candidates. |
| Mixed group | Two exact English copies can coexist with an English color version and a French uncensored version. An exact subgroup stays visible alongside alternatives. |

Persist intentional alternative membership and reasons against stable release and chapter or work IDs. Support language, translation group, color, censorship, edition, quality, version, custom reasons and combinations. Applying an attribute in the workflow saves the same field-level manual override as the release editor, with a bulk path. Keep decisions for keep all, ignored, deferred and reopened states, including reviewer, timestamp, evidence revision and explanation. On subsequent scans exclude unchanged intentional alternatives from the unresolved alternative queue. Keep exact-copy findings independent. Material changes to content, mapping or retained availability invalidate affected plans or reopen the relevant finding with a reason while preserving history.

Reuse `DuplicateReview`, `ChapterComparison`, `DuplicateBatch` and compact archive evidence. Add a library-wide unresolved queue, filters for classification, language, translation group, edition, color and censorship, release facts alongside coverage, sample page comparison and persistent actions. Preserve a stable inspector on hover, highlighting from map and inspector, pointer tooltips, click to select or deselect, visible patterns and colors, and large-series filters and pagination. Compare any number of releases by subgroup and one shared plan. Let the owner keep all, mark alternatives, correct or merge associations, prefer one release or retain several, ignore or defer, reopen, mark selected files for trash and apply approved actions in bulk. One group decision should cover five or more releases without pairwise review.

Before confirmation, calculate availability after the **whole** selected batch, using current retained locations and resolved release, edition and language coverage. Show chapters still available and newly unavailable, language and edition losses, preferred release loss, unique extras or unknown coverage, remaining alternatives and physical space estimated for eventual reclamation. Show before and after contributors. A known unique loss requires a separate explicit warning and confirmation. Surface uncertainty instead of promising equivalence. A standalone file and a volume may cover the same chapter and differ in translations, pages or extras. A filename, cover similarity, weak fingerprint or aggregate chapter count cannot authorize removal. Revalidate each chosen file, observed survivor and plan revision under the operation lock before publication. Unexpected changes require a fresh preview. [Acceptance A03–A08](EXPANDED-ACCEPTANCE.md)

## Trash records and lifecycle

The owner approved explicit placement into managed trash, 30-day default expiration with configurable retention or expiration disabled, a protected-entry option, manual purge of selected entries and manual emptying. Expiration admission starts only after a committed trash entry exists. Discovery, unresolved duplicates and legacy quarantine never enter an expiration sweep by themselves.

Proposed `trash_entry` records preserve root identity and original path, allocated trash location, size, SHA-256 and physical identity when known, removal reason and time, expiration and protection state, associated work, release and chapter/volume coverage snapshot, duplicate decision ID and operation journal link. A tombstone keeps the original provenance, terminal state and timestamps after purge. Keep completion, preferred-release history and associations intelligible even when the last location disappears. The live preferred-release selector reports unavailability.

| Stage | Required behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Plan and admit | Accept catalog location IDs, never an arbitrary filesystem path. Confirm the selected files, unique coverage warnings, route and retention. Fence inaccessible or unqualified roots. Verify object identity and hash, path containment and mount generation. Reject links or reparse points that escape the root. Recheck planned conflicts and authorization at apply. |
| Publish | Prefer a verified no-clobber rename into an application-owned, excluded directory on the same filesystem. For an explicitly supported cross-filesystem route use durable staging, verified size and hash, no-clobber publication, then verified source retirement. Journal each transition and reconcile after a crash. Catalog unavailability and eligible expiration follow verified trash publication. |
| Restore | Restore one or several entries with original-path preview, optional safe alternate destination and no clobber. Check current identity, root permissions and occupancy. Update availability and provenance only after verified publication and reconcile interruption. Restore and purge use the same durable entry claim. |
| Expire or purge | A periodic worker claims due, unprotected, committed entries from the trash table. Recheck eligibility, retention revision, trash root, exact stored location and object identity under the shared claim immediately before removing bytes. Manual purge and emptying use explicit confirmations and a bounded entry snapshot. Neither path traverses active library locations. Journal pending, success or failure and leave a retryable or review record after failure. |
| Recover and clean up | Retain temporary staging or recovery copies until the committed result is verified recoverable through its chosen path. Retire redundant staging and verified superseded recovery copies after reconciliation. Retain one verified recoverable copy during retention wherever possible. Legacy quarantine migrates only after reconciling database row, recovery bytes and identity. Keep older quarantines restorable and protected from expiration until explicitly enrolled. |

The Trash UI shows counts, occupied storage, expiration dates, state and errors. Support individual and batch restore, conflict-safe alternate paths, extend retention, protect/unprotect, manual purge, empty and retention settings. Record resolved deadlines and expose any policy change affecting existing deadlines in a preview. Use the primary's UTC clock and test fake-clock expiration, disabled retention and restart. Decide whether global setting changes apply retroactively during implementation, and test the chosen behavior without shortening a protected entry's deadline silently.

Report active library, trash, temporary staging and long-term recovery/backup bytes separately. Moving to trash delays reclamation. Estimate eventual bytes at the physical object level using actual link counts and observed allocation where supported. Unknown identity stays unknown. Aliases, surviving links, other verified copies retained as recovery material and unavailable roots must not be billed as proven freed space. Verify purge accounting after the operation and keep storage variability explicit.

Qualify no-clobber rename, stable identity, mount fencing, bytes removed and shared restore/purge claims on Windows NTFS, non-root Docker bind mounts and actual Unraid storage. Exercise real Unraid share and pool boundaries and container path mappings. A path prefix cannot establish a same-filesystem move. Where guarantees cannot be established, keep the unsupported mutation disabled with its reason. [Acceptance A09–A13 and A28](EXPANDED-ACCEPTANCE.md) · [Backup scope](UPGRADE-RECOVERY.md)
